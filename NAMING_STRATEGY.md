# Naming & Competitive Strategy — Verified Research

Every claim below was checked via live search, not assumed. Where something wasn't found, that's stated plainly rather than treated as proof of availability.

---

## Names checked and ruled out (7 candidates, all real conflicts)

| Name | Why it's out |
|---|---|
| Bidoro | Two direct, live competitors: scopesnap.app (contractor billing/invoicing app) and scopesnap.io (AI tool turning notes into scopes/estimates). Also an unrelated Mac App Store app. |
| ChalkLine | Chalkline, Inc. — a real construction-software company (BIM/spec document management) serving contractors since 2014. |
| Bidwright | Used by *multiple* live AI construction-estimating products simultaneously (bidwright.com, bidwright.app, an open-source GitHub project). The most saturated hit of all seven. |
| Trady | An established contractor website/CRM/booking platform. (Checking it also surfaced Tradify — see competitor section below.) |
| Estimio | A direct, exact-match competitor already shipped: an app whose entire pitch is "turn job descriptions into professional estimates in under a minute," AI-powered, marketed to contractors and handymen. |
| Quovex | Not in our industry, but it's the live consumer brand of an active forex trading platform ("Quovex — Trade Smarter"). Same name, different industry — usable in theory, but not a clean slate. |
| Trebix | An active real estate/engineering/remodeling company (trebix.com) — adjacent to our space. Also phonetically close to Trellix, a large, currently-active cybersecurity brand (McAfee Enterprise + FireEye), worth avoiding confusion with. |

**One candidate came back clean: Bidoro.** No software, SaaS, or contractor-tool use found anywhere. The only existing uses are unrelated: a traditional Japanese glassware craft term (Tsugaru Bidoro), an African marketplace app, and a small manufacturing-parts company — none in software, none in our category. Worth knowing it's not a fully blank invented word (it has a real, unrelated meaning in Japanese craft), but it's genuinely clear in the category that matters.

**Takeaway worth sitting with:** it took seven attempts to find one clean name. That's real evidence the "AI + contractor quoting" space is already crowded — useful market validation (real demand, multiple funded/serious competitors), but it means the name alone won't differentiate us. The strategy below has to.

## What we can do that none of them do

1. **We're the only one with a genuine one-time price.** Every competitor found is subscription-based, several costing more per month than our entire one-time price. A solo contractor pays us once and it's less than a single month of Tradify's cheapest tier.

2. **We're the only one where the AI itself costs nothing, to anyone, ever.** Tradify has to gate its AI features behind its most expensive tier because running AI costs *them* money at scale. Ours runs on the buyer's own free API key — there's no cost for us to eventually raise a price on, because there's no marginal cost to begin with.

3. **We're the only one with no account and no lock-in.** Every competitor here is a cloud platform you log into. Ours is a single file the buyer owns outright — nothing to lose access to, no future price hike risk, no vendor holding their data hostage.

4. **We're deliberately narrow, not a whole business OS.** Tradify and Bidwright are both trying to be complete platforms — scheduling, timesheets, purchase orders, subcontractor management. That's real value for a growing crew, but genuine overkill (and overpriced) for a solo operator who mainly loses time on one specific step: turning a customer's text into a quote fast. We do that one thing well instead of ten things adequately.

**The positioning line this points to:** *"The $49 quoting tool for solo contractors who don't need — or want to pay monthly for — a whole business platform."*

---

## Recommendation

Move forward with **Bidoro**, on the strength of a genuinely clean check in our category — with the honest caveat that it's an unusual word with real (unrelated) meaning elsewhere, not a fully invented one. If that doesn't sit right on a gut level, say so — the next move would be checking 2–3 more fully invented candidates before committing, not settling because we're tired of searching.
